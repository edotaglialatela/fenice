Physics - viscous fluxes, gradients, body forces, IBM

Cell-centre gradients

compute_gradients evaluates central differences on the BC-aware padded state:

$$\left.\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right|_i = \frac{\phi_{i+1} - \phi_{i-1}}{2\Delta x}$$

(compute_gradients_3d adds $\partial/\partial z$ on 3-D meshes).

On a uniform orthogonal grid this is identical to both the Green-Gauss face average and the least-squares reconstruction. Near walls the stencil uses the diffusive ghost cells, so wall velocities enter the gradients correctly.

Navier-Stokes viscous residual

For a Newtonian fluid under Stokes' hypothesis the viscous stress tensor is

$$\tau_{xx} = \mu\Bigl(\tfrac43\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} - \tfrac23\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\Bigr), \quad \tau_{yy} = \mu\Bigl(\tfrac43\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} - \tfrac23\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\Bigr), \quad \tau_{xy} = \mu\Bigl(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\Bigr)$$

In 3-D (viscous_residual_3d) the full tensor is used,

$$\tau_{ij} = \mu\Bigl(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i} - \tfrac23\,\delta_{ij}\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\Bigr)$$

which reduces to the expressions above when $w = 0$ and nothing varies along z.

The Fourier heat flux uses the conductivity $k = \mu\gamma/[\Pr(\gamma-1)]$:

$$\mathbf q = -k\,\nabla T, \qquad T = p/\rho$$

The residual is the divergence of the viscous flux, assembled face by face: $[0,\ \tau_{xx},\ \tau_{xy},\ u\tau_{xx} + v\tau_{xy} + w\tau_{xz} - q_x,\ \tau_{xz}]$ on x-faces and the analogues on y- and z-faces, scaled by the face area and divided by the cell volume.

Stencils.

  • Normal derivatives use a compact 2-point difference across the face, $(\phi_{i+1} - \phi_i)/\Delta x$. This gives a proper 3-point Laplacian, which damps the velocity checkerboard mode.
  • Cross derivatives, such as $\partial^2u/\partial x\partial y$, use the face average of the cell-centre gradients.
  • At walls, the ghost velocities and temperatures come from the padded state. The wall shear is therefore correct for no-slip walls, and the adiabatic ghost temperature gives zero wall heat flux.
  • Across an MPI interface, the neighbour's gradients are rebuilt from the halo (CrossGhosts), so the stencil matches the serial run.
  • At a periodic boundary, the cross derivatives use the opposite edge cell. The stencil is then the same as in the interior, and a periodic domain is exactly translation-invariant (checked bit for bit in 2-D and 3-D).

Time-step limit. The von Neumann condition of the heat equation gives

$$\Delta t_{\rm visc} = \frac14\,\frac{\min(\Delta x, \Delta y)^2}{\max(\nu, \alpha_{\rm th})}\ \ \text{(2-D)}, \qquad \Delta t_{\rm visc} = \frac{1}{2\max(\nu, \alpha_{\rm th})\sum_d \Delta x_d^{-2}}\ \ \text{(3-D)}$$

with $\nu = \mu/\rho_{\min}$ and $\alpha_{\rm th} = k/(\rho_{\min} c_p)$. On square/cubic cells these are $h^2/4\nu$ and $h^2/6\nu$, the forward-Euler limits of the 2-D and 3-D heat equation. The limit enters the CFL estimate as an equivalent speed $S_{\rm visc} = \mathrm{CFL}\,h/\Delta t_{\rm visc}$, with the same length $h$ as the convective step (see Overview).

Body forces

ConstantBodyForce adds a uniform acceleration $\mathbf g = (g_x, g_y, g_z)$ (gravity or an imposed pressure gradient; $g_z$ acts only on 3-D meshes):

$$\mathbf S = \bigl[0,\ \rho g_x,\ \rho g_y,\ \rho\,\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf g,\ \rho g_z\bigr]^\top$$

The energy component is the power delivered by the force, $\mathbf u\cdot\rho\mathbf g$.

Immersed boundary method

A direct-forcing IBM with a circular obstacle. A cell is solid when its centre lies inside the shape. In 2-D Cylinder and Sphere are the same circle. In 3-D, Sphere is a ball centred at (ibm_cx, ibm_cy, ibm_cz), and Cylinder is a circular cylinder running through the whole depth along z.

  • Forcing (apply_forcing) runs inside every residual evaluation, so it applies at every Runge-Kutta stage. It zeroes the velocity of solid cells and leaves density and pressure untouched, which keeps the multi-stage step thermodynamically consistent.
  • Solid reset (apply_solid_reset) runs once per complete time step:
    • Fringe cells are solid cells with at least one fluid neighbour. They take $\rho$ and $p$ from that neighbour (a Neumann, zero-gradient condition). This suppresses the pressure well that would otherwise form at the surface, and avoids artificial mass sources at the boundary.
    • Deep solid cells have no fluid neighbour. They are frozen to $(\rho_{\rm ref}, p_{\rm ref}) = $ (rho_in, p_out) to stop long-term drift.
    • The velocity is zero throughout the obstacle.

The reset must never run inside RK stages. Doing so introduces hard density and pressure discontinuities, which the Riemann solver amplifies into spurious shocks. Under MPI the fringe test sees the neighbours across rank interfaces (SolidResetHalo), and the mask is cut from the global mask, so the obstacle is identical for any number of ranks. The 3-D fringe test also looks at the z-neighbours.
